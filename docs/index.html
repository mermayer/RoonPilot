04

From first boot to Roon control

A clean factory image contains no private Wi-Fi or Roon data.

  1. 1

    Join the setup AP

    Connect to RoonPilot-Setup-XXXXXX. If the captive page does not open, visit 192.168.4.1.

  2. 2

    Connect Wi-Fi

    Select or enter your 2.4 GHz network and save the password locally on the device.

  3. 3

    Open the local UI

    Use the address shown by your router or the device. No Internet account is required.

  4. 4

    Approve RoonPilot

    In Roon, open Settings → Extensions and enable RoonPilot by Senior Coder.

  5. 5

    Select the server

    If several Roon Servers are found, choose the intended one. A fixed local IP can be entered when discovery is blocked.

  6. 6

    Choose your zones

    Select the primary zone and hide rooms that should not appear on the device.

Wrong Wi-Fi password? After about 45 seconds RoonPilot returns to its setup AP, so the credentials can be corrected without erasing or reflashing.

Tactile control. Beautifully simple.

Turn the full outer ring for volume. RoonPilot automatically follows dimensionless, dB and relative outputs without inventing a unit. Tap, swipe or hold the capacitive display for playback and device controls.

  • Optional artwork-sized centre Play/Pause touch area
  • Optional Roon playlists, Live Radio, Power and Mute shortcuts

06

One Roon group. Every physical output under control.

RoonPilot preserves the volume route of every group member instead of treating unlike devices as one anonymous endpoint.

No extra on-screen button

The first detent opens the mixer. The next movement controls the music.

A Roon group may combine a native numeric endpoint, a real dB endpoint, relative Roon volume and several IR-controlled devices. RoonPilot sends the correct command to each physical output and displays every result in its honest unit—such as 48, −35.0 dB and IR +2 side by side.

Tap a generously sized member row to adjust only that output. Tap the group heading to return to the complete group. The mixer closes automatically after eight seconds of inactivity.

Read the complete group guide
First detent is safe

It opens the mixer without changing volume—even when it also wakes a black display.

Up to three touch rows

The round display shows three selectable members; whole-group control still processes every eligible output.

Several Bridges at once

One BLE path and several authenticated Wi-Fi paths can serve the same active Roon group.

Failures stay local

One offline route does not silently move to another Bridge or stop healthy group members.

07

Optional IR control, assigned zone by zone.

RoonPilot remains the only user interface while a small ESP32-S3 Bridge reproduces the original infrared commands beside the audio hardware.

One firmware, optional hardware

Control the DAC itself when Roon cannot.

Each physical Roon output independently selects native Roon volume, an external IR Bridge/profile or deliberately disabled volume. Up to four Bridge pairings can be saved. Automatic zone control follows the selected zone and, for a Roon group, monitors and operates every Bridge required by its members.

Pairing and all sensitive provisioning start over encrypted Bluetooth LE. Authenticated local Wi-Fi is optional for another room and is preferred for a confirmed firmware transfer. Pairing, profiles, learning, routing, diagnostics, backup and signed A/B updates are all managed by RoonPilot—there is no separate Bridge app, cloud account or everyday web page.

Example: WiiM Ultra and RME ADI-2 DAC. Playback continues through Roon and WiiM, while the ring sends learned RME volume commands. The DAC performs its own hardware level change and retains Auto Ref Level instead of substituting Roon digital attenuation.

04

Completely optional

Switch Bridge & Bluetooth off and restart: no Bluetooth, scans, Bridge traffic or Bridge update checks start.

No server bridge. No Raspberry Pi. No additional service.

RoonPilot talks directly to Roon on your local network. The optional IR Bridge is hardware control at the equipment—not a required host or cloud service.

A printable stand with an integrated USB-C position.

Four supplied STL parts hold RoonPilot at a comfortable angle while the mounting photographs document the connector position inside the cradle.

Optional 3D-print project

Print the body, bottom cover, USB-C holder and rear panel once each.

The model does not change RoonPilot and is not needed for operation. A thin foam strip selected for the firmness of the material guides the right-angle magnetic USB-C coupler from below without locking it rigidly. The holder above it is secured removably with two small screws so the coupler can be aligned without mechanical stress.
